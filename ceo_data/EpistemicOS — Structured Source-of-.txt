EpistemicOS — Structured Source-of-Truth Dataset
EpistemicOS is a B2B SaaS platform for epistemic validation and reviewer-readiness assessment of academic manuscripts.
The product operates as a pre-submission research verification layer that evaluates whether a paper’s claims are:
supported by appropriate evidence,
methodologically justified,
inferentially coherent,
traceable to sources,
and aligned with reviewer and journal expectations.
The system works at the claim level rather than the document level. It extracts claims, classifies claim types, maps evidence, evaluates inference validity, identifies methodological and theoretical weaknesses, detects citation-support gaps, and surfaces reviewer-facing risks before submission.
EpistemicOS is not designed as a generative writing assistant or autonomous peer-review replacement system. Its primary function is epistemic auditability and structured research validation.
Initial domain focus:
management research,
business schools,
doctoral research,
interdisciplinary social science environments.
Long-term positioning:
epistemic infrastructure for trustworthy academic knowledge production.

Secondary Buyer Hypothesis
Individual researchers and doctoral students.
Value drivers:
pre-submission diagnostics,

claim/evidence validation support.
This segment may function as:
early adoption channel,

Tertiary Buyer Hypothesis
Academic journals and publishers.
Potential use cases:

reviewer augmentation,

auditability workflows,
structured manuscript diagnostics.
This is likely a later-stage market due to:
procurement complexity,
workflow integration requirements,
reputational and governance sensitivity.

Current Strategic Assumption Set
Current architecture implicitly assumes:
pre-submission workflow,
institutional primary buyer,
researcher operational user,
SaaS subscription model,
Spain/EU-first GTM,
MVP-stage product maturity,
trust/auditability positioning rather than productivity positioning.
These assumptions should be treated as working hypotheses requiring empirical validation rather than fixed truths.

The largest unresolved uncertainty is now:
whether epistemic validation is perceived as sufficiently valuable to become a repeated institutional workflow behavior rather than an interesting research-quality concept.
